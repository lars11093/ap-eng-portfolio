Year 1 of ICT English is finished, and the honest summary is that I got a decent
grade for work that was not very good. I passed by writing the minimum, close to
the deadline, in language I already knew was safe. That works until it does not.
This year I would rather do the harder version.

## What actually went wrong last year

Three things, and none of them were about vocabulary.

The first was timing. I wrote most of my Year 1 entries in the last two weeks
before they were due. Reflection written four months after the lesson is not
reflection — it is reconstruction. I could not remember what I had found
difficult, so I wrote what sounded reasonable instead.

The second was safety. When I was not sure how to say something, I said something
simpler that I was sure about. Over a whole year that adds up to writing which is
technically correct and says almost nothing. My Year 1 portfolio has a lot of
sentences like "It was interesting and I learned a lot."

The third was speaking. I avoided it. In group work I let the people with more
confident English do the presenting, and I took the parts where I could hide
behind a slide. I got away with it, which is exactly the problem.

## Where I think I am now

Rather than guess, I checked my writing against the CEFR global scale. On
writing I am somewhere around B2: I can produce clear, detailed text on subjects
in my field, and I can explain a technical point of view. On spontaneous speaking
I am closer to B1, because I lose fluency as soon as I am not reading from
something I prepared.

That gap between my writing and my speaking is the honest picture, and it matches
what happened in class.

## Three goals for this year

**Write each entry within a week of the lesson.** Not for the deadline — while I
still remember what confused me. If the entry is late, the reflection in it is
fiction.

**Say the difficult version.** When I catch myself simplifying a sentence because
I am not sure of the grammar, I will write the harder sentence and mark it, then
check it afterwards. Being wrong in a marked way is more useful than being safe.

**Speak first in group work at least once per module.** Volunteering to present
is the only part of this I genuinely do not want to do, which is a reasonable
sign that it is the part I need.

I will come back to this entry at the end of the school year and check these three
against what I actually did.
